Where Capybaras Thrive

A habitat suitability model for the capybara (Hydrochoerus hydrochaeris) across South America, combining river density, climate, landform, and elevation into one weighted suitability surface.

Course GIS 250: Intro GIS & Spatial Analysis, BYU-Idaho Term Spring 2024 Software ArcGIS Pro

Key findings

  • The most suitable habitat sits in low to mid-elevation areas with high river density, especially the Amazon Basin and the Pantanal wetlands.
  • The top-scoring regions combine abundant water sources with favorable land cover.
  • The model flagged potential conservation and expansion areas both inside and outside the species' current known range.

The question

Which habitat features define where capybaras live?

Capybaras are semi-aquatic grazers tied to rivers, marshes, and seasonally flooded lowlands. This project translated those habitat needs into a spatial model: score each landscape factor by how well it supports capybaras, weight the factors by importance, and combine them into a single suitability map to compare against the species' known range.

The original proposal also set out to map habitat overlap with the capybara's predators: jaguars, green anacondas, and spectacled caimans. The final project focused on capybara habitat itself.

How each factor was rated

Method

Analysis workflow

  1. Clip to the study range

    Clipped river features and other habitat data to the area where capybaras are known to live, using Clip.

  2. Map the raw habitat features

    Built initial layouts of elevation, rivers, bioclimate, and land cover before any reclassification.

  3. Reclassify for suitability

    Used Reclassify to convert the elevation, land cover, and climate rasters to the shared suitability scale.

  4. Derive river density

    Ran Line Density on the river network, reclassified the result to the same value range, and clipped it to the capybara range.

  5. Standardize symbology

    Applied a continuous blue ramp to river density and a red–yellow–green ramp to every suitability layer, so all maps read the same way.

  6. Combine with a Weighted Sum

    Combined the reclassified layers with Weighted Sum into one habitat suitability surface.

  7. Compare against the known range

    Mapped the reclassified layers before clipping, overlaid the capybara range, and examined suitability scores outside it to find potential expansion and conservation areas.

Beyond the range

Looking south of the known range

The analysis also examined habitat features and suitability scores outside the current range, to identify regions with potential for range expansion or conservation. These maps show elevation and landforms across southern South America.
